A global retailer's first digital waitlist launch

A high-visibility launch for a global fashion retailer. My job was to see the risks before peak traffic, keep escalation one message away, and own everything that came after.

Role
Customer Success Manager, account owner
When
May 2026
Areas
Search, catalog, checkout, marketplace

The situation

A global fashion retailer was running its first digital waitlist launch for a high-demand collection. That means a lot of traffic concentrated in a few minutes, on an account where a bad launch day would be visible to the client's global leadership.

The problem

Launches like this tend to fail exactly at the peak, when there is no time to find the right person. The usual answer is a preventive incident with a war room, which costs the whole team a day.

Launch, end to end

  1. Before launchTook ownership, looped in Product and Engineering, pushed for a preventive posture
  2. Launch dayOn site with the client as the escalation path
  3. PeakSeveral times the normal peak in sales
  4. AfterFollow-through and the quarterly business review

What I did

  1. Took ownership nobody had assigned

    The launch had no clear owner on our side. Instead of waiting for the request to find its way, I messaged Product and Engineering directly, opened the internal workflows myself, and pushed for a preventive war-room posture before peak traffic.

  2. Became the escalation path

    Leadership declined the war room rather than tie up the whole team for a day. The plan was me instead: with me on the account, escalation to Engineering was one message away for the entire launch.

  3. Stayed on site with the client

    On launch day I was on site with the client for the whole launch, so any issue could go from their team to ours in a single step. It mattered: a few products set up at the last minute weren't showing as expected. I got the fix prioritized with Engineering on the spot, and the launch went on as planned.

  4. Owned the follow-through

    After the launch I took the loose ends to resolution: a merchandising ranking issue, product page attributes, a payment workaround and marketplace unblocks.

  5. Told the story back

    I wrote the quarterly business review with the launch results, which were shared with the client's global leadership.

What changed

  • Sales ran several times above the normal peak on day one, and the execution ran smoother than previous launches.
  • I stayed on the account through the quarter, which closed with double-digit growth in revenue and average order value.
