From support tickets to a product map

Support tickets from the largest accounts were the best record of where the platform hurt them, and nobody was reading them as a whole. I turned a quarter of them into a product map that Product leadership asked to send to Engineering for diagnosis.

Role
Customer Success Manager, bridging to Product
When
October to November 2026
Tools
Claude Code, support ticket data
Audience
Product leadership

The situation

In October my role changed: instead of a portfolio of my own, I started supporting the whole Brazilian team's top-tier accounts and bridging them to Product. Product wanted to know where those accounts were struggling, and every account team had its own anecdotes.

The problem

The tickets were the record, but they were thousands of free-text conversations across a couple of hundred accounts, filed under support categories that don't match how Product is organized. Read one by one, they say "this account has a problem". Read together, they could say "this part of the product has a problem", and that is the sentence Product can act on.

From the whole quarter to what matters

  1. Thousands of tickets from the top-tier accounts, one quarter
  2. Kept the ones in scope
  3. Classified into about twenty product verticals
  4. A handful of verticals, classified again by problem and outcome

Not to scale.

What I did

  1. Mapped tickets to the product, not to support

    I pulled every ticket from the top-tier accounts over the quarter, kept the ones in scope, and classified them into about twenty product verticals, the way Product thinks about the platform rather than the way support files a ticket.

  2. Went deep where the volume was

    A handful of verticals concentrated most of the volume. For the biggest ones I classified each ticket again by type of problem and how it ended, so the map said not only where, but what kind of trouble and whether it got solved.

  3. Checked the AI before trusting it

    The classification ran with Claude Code, so before anything left my hands I had the findings fact-checked independently, claim by claim. The ones that didn't hold were corrected before delivery. A product map that is confidently wrong is worse than no map.

  4. Brought it down to one sentence

    The sentence I led with for Product leadership:

    Top-tier accounts open several times more marketplace tickets than the average account.

What changed

  • A Product leader said that in years at the company he had never had a conversation like that built on tickets, and asked for the report to go to Engineering for diagnosis.
  • The Product AI team asked for the underlying data for their own analysis, and Product proposed a pilot to run the whole support base through AI.
  • My manager asked me to keep the reports going and to help turn the roadmap into a view for the account team.
